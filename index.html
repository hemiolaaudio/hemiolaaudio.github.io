14 HP

Cycle Clock

A clock that counts a whole rhythm cycle, such as a 16-beat thalam or a 7/8 bar, and tells your patch where it is.

  • 55 cycles: ordinary meters, 7 Hindustani and 35 Carnatic thalams
  • Trigger and gate on every beat, section and cycle
  • Position voltage, accented click, swing, tempo CV
Cycle Clock manual

10 HP

R&T, Rhythm & Thalam

Splits every beat into equal parts, the way Carnatic music counts nadai, then divides those parts.

  • 1 to 9 parts per beat: tisra, chatusra, khanda, misra, sankirna
  • DIVIDE from 1 to 64, with its own outputs
  • Chance, reset input, division by CV
R&T manual

Get them

  1. Download the latest file ending in .vcvplugin from the releases page. It is built for a Mac with Apple silicon.
  2. Put it in the plugins-mac-arm64 folder inside Documents/Rack2, then start Rack.
  3. Other computers: the plugin has been submitted to the VCV Library, which builds it for Windows, Mac and Linux. You can also build it yourself.

Two example patches are in the patches folder.

Three against two

A hemiola is the moment three beats lean against two. Rhythms that should not fit, and somehow do, are where this project began.

Hemiola Audio is one person. I make music in VCV Rack most days, and I kept reaching for things that did not quite exist: a clock that knew it was on beat eleven of sixteen, and a way to cut a beat into five, seven or nine equal parts, the way a Carnatic musician does without thinking.

I am not a trained programmer. I write the code with an AI assistant, and I decide everything else: what a module is for, how it looks, how it feels under the hands. Every timing rule is checked by machine. Whether it is musical is checked the old way, in a patch, with headphones on.

The modules are free and open source, and they will stay that way. More are coming, and not all of them will be about rhythm. Read the longer version.
